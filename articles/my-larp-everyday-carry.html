MY LARP-EVERYDAY CARRY

Published 2026-11-06 · 3 min read

This is the stuff I actually use for class, coding, and whatever else I get up to. Nothing here is perfect, but it all works for me, and that's what matters.

My Laptop

Acer Nitro V 15 (ANV15-51-541P)

My main machine is the Acer Nitro V 15 (ANV15-51-541P). It came with an Intel Core i5-13420H, an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4050 Laptop GPU at 70 W with 6 GB of VRAM, and 16 GB of Samsung DDR5-5200 RAM, with one of the two slots used. It also came with a 512 GB NVMe drive. I used the second M.2 slot to add a 2 TB Kingston Gen 4 SSD, so storage is definitely not something I'm short on.

The i5-13420H is a hybrid 8-core, 12-thread CPU: four Performance-cores with Hyper-Threading and four Efficient-cores. The P-cores can boost up to 4.6 GHz, while the E-cores reach up to 3.4 GHz. Intel gives it a 45 W processor base power and a 95 W maximum turbo power, though those are limits rather than a promise that it will sit at either wattage all the time. For my usual work, that mix is plenty: web coding, running an editor and browser together, and everyday multitasking are not a problem. The extra cores also help when I have more going on, but long, heavy builds or lots of demanding apps will put more pressure on cooling and memory.

The screen is 1080p at 165 Hz, which is nice, but the colors aren't great: around 45% NTSC / 66% sRGB. That's probably my biggest issue with the laptop. I'd like to swap it for a better panel with 100% sRGB / 75% NTSC. I've found a couple that should work, the BOE NV156FHM-NY8 and AUO B156HAN12.H, so maybe that'll happen eventually.

It's not thin, but it's not a huge brick either, at least to me. I like the ports too: a headphone jack, three USB ports, Thunderbolt (the Intel models have it; I don't think the AMD ones do), and RJ45 Ethernet. It doesn't overheat much in normal use. When I play something heavier like Red Dead Redemption 2, the fans ramp up and get pretty loud, but it cools down and the noise doesn't really bother me.

For Coding and Everyday Use

For coding, the CPU is doing most of the work; the RTX 4050 is mostly a bonus unless an app or tool can make use of it. HTML, CSS, and JavaScript don't ask for much, and I can have my editor, browser, and local dev tools open without the laptop struggling. The CPU has enough headroom for bigger projects and more multitasking too. RAM is the thing I run into first: 16 GB is fine for what I do, but 32 GB would've been better, especially if I start running virtual machines or heavier tools. That's on the list for later.

The battery obviously isn't MacBook-level, but I've done better than the up-to-six-hours the listing claims. With light coding, browsing, and writing documents, I can get around seven to eight hours. If I'm using it heavily, it's more like four to five. Not amazing, but good enough for me.

For Gaming

The RTX 4050 Laptop GPU is based on NVIDIA's Ada Lovelace architecture. It has 2,560 CUDA cores for regular graphics work, 20 ray-tracing cores, and 80 Tensor cores for features like DLSS. Mine has 6 GB of GDDR6 on a 96-bit memory bus and is configured for up to 70 W. Laptop GPU clocks and power limits vary between machines, so the name "RTX 4050" by itself doesn't tell the whole performance story. This one is a good fit for the 1080p games I play, but the 6 GB of VRAM and 70 W power limit can become the bottleneck in newer, demanding games, especially with high-resolution textures or ray tracing. That's when I lower settings or use an optimized preset instead of trying to max everything out.

I'm not a hardcore gamer. Most of what I play barely makes the GPU work: Terraria, Blasphemous, Warhammer: Vermintide 2, and Deep Rock Galactic. For The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt, I used a console-style graphics preset. I could barely tell the difference, and it ran a lot smoother, so that was an easy choice.

My Peripherals

CMF Buds 2a

I wasn't expecting much from the CMF Buds 2a since they were so cheap, but I like them more than I thought I would. The case is plastic, obviously, but the design and overall quality are still nice, and the bass is solid. The ANC isn't perfect, but it does blur out enough background noise for me. Battery life is really good, and charging them doesn't take long. I kind of wish I'd gone for the Pro since it wasn't that much more, but I'm still happy with these.

Logitech MX Vertical

I fell for the vertical mouse propaganda and got the Logitech MX Vertical. Turns out, I kind of dig it. I've always had big hands, and my old Shark Attack X11 was light but a bit small. The MX fits my hand way better. It's not a cheap mouse, and it feels premium; it's pretty heavy, but I don't mind that. The battery is no joke either. I've used it for a month and still haven't charged it. I draw with my mouse a lot, and somehow this shape makes that more comfortable. Bringing it to class is also a bit of a head-turner.

Aula F75

Creamy keyboards are my weakness, and the Aula F75 hits that spot. It's heavy, but it feels really premium for an affordable keyboard. Mine was second-hand and still feels great. I like the sound and the feel, and honestly, I don't have anything to complain about.

That's my setup. The laptop gets me through class, coding, and games, and the rest are things I just enjoy using. It's not perfect, but I like it, and I wouldn't swap any of it out right now.
